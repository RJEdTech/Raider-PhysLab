    For teachers: what this pilot is, the model, and sources

    This is a trial of a different lesson design for Forces, aimed at the idea RJ Physics tests show students miss most: net force is not the same thing as motion, and zero net force does not mean stopped.

    • One story. A shopping cart, shoved once and let go, then a row of carts, the job many students have had or will have.
    • Students commit first, and build. They tap the forces they believe act on the rolling cart, hold a cart at steady speed by dragging their own push, and draw the speed line for a cart on ice before they see it.
    • Linked pictures. The arrows on the cart become the free-body diagram, the leftover becomes the net force, and the net force sets the slope of the speed line.
    • A real case. Voyager 1, which reached one light-day from Earth on November 18, 2026.

    Ways to use it. Project step 1 and have the class vote on each arrow. Step 7 (draw the line) works well as a turn-and-talk before you reveal step 8. Or assign it and collect “Tell it back” with Copy for Canvas. The arrow keys move between steps.

    The model. One cart is 25 kg with 10 N of rolling friction, the same whether it is starting or moving. The shove is 60 N for 1 s. So the net force is 50 N, the cart reaches 2 m/s, and it coasts 5 m in 5 s.

    • 25 kg is a round number for a full-size steel cart; weigh one for the real figure. 10 N of friction is a rolling-resistance coefficient of about 0.04. Nested carts are drawn 0.27 m apart, the nesting depth one maker lists (10.8 in).
    • Gravity on the cart is 25 kg × 9.8 = 245 N. The up and down arrows are drawn to the same scale as the sideways ones until the diagram zooms in, and it says so when it does.
    • Voyager 1: launch date, speed (38,000 mph, 17 km/s) and the one light-day date are NASA’s (NASA Science, NASA SVS). The speed curve since 1980 is my calculation from the Sun’s gravity alone, tuned to today’s speed and distance. It is not NASA tracking data. Small thrusters still turn the spacecraft; they do not drive it forward.
